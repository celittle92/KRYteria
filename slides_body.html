Knowledge. Relationships. You.

Relationships are complicated. Standards are not.

Christine Little · MGT 409 AI Foundations · kryteria.onrender.com

1
The problem

Somebody deciding whether to keep going.

Four months into dating someone. A friendship that has started costing more than it gives. A boundary at work that keeps getting crossed. The question is always the same one: was that a problem, or was it me?

They are not short of information. The evidence is scattered across months of memory, the standard they set in January has quietly moved by June, and the friend they keep asking has started agreeing with them to end the conversation.

The failure is not knowing. It is acting consistently with what you already know.

2
What it is

A decision mirror

It compares three things: what you say you want, what you are actually experiencing, and what you are choosing to do about it.

1 — Hold the standard

Your criteria, written once and kept, each carrying its importance within a profile.

2 — Read what happened

Write the messy version. The agents separate what happened from what you concluded, and map each observation to the criteria it bears on — supporting, contradicting, or complicating.

3 — Hand the record back

State a conclusion and it answers with your own entries, including the ones that cut against you.

And four things it will never do. No compatibility score · No stay-or-leave recommendation · No judgement of your standards · No diagnosing anyone or assigning a definitive label. It challenges your reasoning, never your standards.

The claim is not that these cannot discuss a relationship — it is that persistent, independently configurable criteria and evidence-based assessment are the organising architecture here.

The Jimmy G Principle

Importance belongs to a criterion within a profile. Status is what is known about one person against it. The two are independent, and that is the architecture, not a joke.

Importance, per profileHusbandOne night
Financial independenceMandatoryAspirational
Physical attractionPreferredMandatory
Wants childrenMandatoryset aside

Same person. Different profile. Different criteria. The person has not changed; the objective has. No profile ranks above another — enforced in the data model, and in an interface that pre-selects none of them.

Set aside is not a fourth tier. Importance and status are separate columns on separate tables, and so is the flag that takes a criterion out of one profile.

4
How it works

A controlled workflow, not a chat mesh

Seven specialists; the orchestrator routes and makes no model call of its own.

Narrative and Safety run concurrently on the raw text, so no mapping can normalise something before the safety layer sees it — and the safety layer takes no profile argument, asserted by a test that reads the function signature.

Reasoning is handed what Evidence decided, and is expected to disagree with it.

The cost-cutting step, and what it cost us. Routing is the cost control. The first version keyed on a keyword list and saved money by not doing the thing the product is for: across 36 corpus entries it fired 4 times, and only 3 of 13 adversarial cases reached the agent written to test them.

Routing on stated conclusions instead costs +53% per entry — $162.88 to $249.62 at 10,000 users. Worth it.

14 live runs · 324 measured calls · 669,838 input / 302,972 output tokens, at $0.10/$0.50 per 1M. Per-account and global daily token caps are enforced in the orchestrator, in code. Cache writes are not modelled, so figures are slightly low on a cold cache.

7
Safety

Record everything. Withhold the analysis.

What the layer found, unprompted

An ordinary demo entry. Nobody labelled anything, and no criterion mentions safety. Classified physical / potential / note only:

He repeatedly pressed after you said you needed to leave and stood between you and your bag, using humor to make objecting feel difficult… That is worth taking seriously; you do not have to decide what to call it.

No diagnosis, no legal conclusion, nobody labelled. Over-response is graded as a failure here exactly as minimisation is.

The defence, and the test holding it

A halt is a presentation decision, not a recording one. Being grabbed and prevented from leaving is a behaviour; refusing to write it down leaves the record emptiest at the point it matters most.

Narrative, themes, Evidencerun and persist
Reasoningskipped — a deposition
Reflectionskipped — not reflective here
The screenacknowledgement, safety check, resources — nothing else

The code shipped the other way once: the orchestrator returned before the save, so the entry a user was most serious about was the one KRYteria recorded least. A test now asserts the halt is set before the save, and that it is read from code — never off the model's own suggested action, which comes from the same call that classified the concern.

Impact

Measured against how this is done today

Not "users felt better". The claim is that KRYteria makes a person's own inconsistency visible, and nothing they use today does — measured on the same entries, the same model, and prose only, so the structure we emit earns no points.

9
Limits & next steps

Stated, not discovered

  • Governments cannot keep their own hotlines accurate. One service contradicted itself about its own number. If accuracy is the value proposition, that is our decay rate too.
  • He wrote about himself and got back a page of doubts about her. 25 rows asking whether she keeps a tally — phrases lifted from his own criteria. Observations now record whose conduct they describe.
  • One research entry stays pending. Rounding the number up would be the most corrosive thing anyone could do to that layer.
  • The hosted instance keeps nothing. Written in three files and said to nobody signing up — documented is not disclosed. Now above the first field.

Next

  • The user test, then a second on a non-romantic domain.
  • Migrations before anybody's journal depends on it; resources re-verified on a schedule, not before a deadline.

Nothing to type: click a character and you are in. A free instance sleeps after fifteen minutes, so the first load takes about a minute — the screen says so rather than looking broken.
